Climate Science Context Guide

Understanding how true observations can lead to misleading conclusions

1. The Water Vapor Trap

The Claim: "Global warming is caused by water vapor, not humans, because water vapor is the most abundant greenhouse gas."

What's True
Water vapor is indeed the most plentiful greenhouse gas. It traps a massive amount of heat in our atmosphere organically.
The Scientific Context
Water vapor doesn't act on its own; it acts as an amplifier. Human CO₂ emissions turn up the global thermostat. Because warmer air holds more moisture, water vapor levels rise and double the warming effect.

2. The Ice Sheet Paradox

The Claim: "Ice sheets are actually growing larger, meaning the planet isn't really warming."

What's True
Parts of East Antarctica have experienced localized mass gains in recent decades due to heavy and frequent snowstorms.
The Scientific Context
Climate change directly drives this extra snowfall. Because human emissions create a warmer and wetter atmosphere, the air holds more moisture, triggering severe snowstorms over East Antarctica. However, these isolated local gains do not offset the massive, catastrophic ice losses happening across West Antarctica and Greenland. Globally, Earth loses billions of tons of ice annually.

3. The Global Greening Illusion

The Claim: "The planet is becoming much greener, which proves that rising CO₂ is good for nature."

What's True
Satellite images do show an increase in green foliage over the last few decades, partially caused by plants growing faster due to higher atmospheric carbon levels.
The Scientific Context
A "greener" planet does not equal a healthier ecosystem. This CO₂ fertilization effect is a temporary reaction to an unstable climate. The slight benefit of extra leaf growth is heavily wiped out by climate complications, including extreme droughts, severe heatwaves, destructive wildfires, and changing weather patterns.
